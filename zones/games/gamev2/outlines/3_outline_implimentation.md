### **Part 1: The Final Terminal Interaction (The 1000-Key Trap)**

To make the "Autopsy of Intellect" hit with maximum devastation, the player’s interaction with the final terminal cannot be a simple button press or a quick-time event. It must be a grueling, highly complex intellectual task that triggers their "earned dogmatism". They must feel like they are applying Isla's "Kinetic Decoding" and Ethel's mathematical brilliance to break the system.

**The Interaction Mechanics:**

* **The Overload:** When the player accesses the terminal to "rescue" the silent entity, the screen fills with raw, scrolling hexadecimal dumps, mimicking a massive data server.
* **The Puzzle:** The player is presented with a brutal logic puzzle. They must manually match 14 distinct, encrypted data strings (representing Dominic's firewall wardens) to corresponding port addresses using a cipher they had to decode earlier in the level. It requires them to cross-reference notes, check UI logs, and physically type the decryption commands on their keyboard.
* **The "Flow" State:** As they successfully bypass each of the 14 wardens, the UI flashes positive reinforcement. Progress bars fill. The system *feels* like it is yielding to their superior intellect.
* **The Trap:** This entire puzzle is the equivalent of the "one thousand keys" scenario. The player is rigorously debugging the firewall as if it were a flawed piece of code keeping a victim trapped. By making the mechanical execution highly demanding, you completely consume their cognitive load. They are so focused on *how* to break the lock that they completely forget to ask *why* the lock is there in the first place.

They don't realize they are executing a virus until they hit `ENTER` on the 14th command, locking in their moral complicity.

### **Part 2: Ethel’s "Nexus Map" UI (The Illusion of Validity)**

Earlier in the game, the player needs a tool to navigate the financial empire of Dominic Ryker. You give them Ethel’s masterpiece: the "Targeted Asset and Liability Matrix". This UI element is the player's primary intellectual weapon, and it must look cold, objective, and mathematically flawless.

**Visual Design (Rendered in Vulkan):**

* **The Aesthetic:** It should look like high-end data science software. Stark, monochromatic (perhaps deep wireframe blues and clinical whites), with no unnecessary ornamentation. It is pure forensic architecture.
* **The Nodes:** The UI visualizes Dominic's 200 properties. Each property is a node.
* **The Shell Entity Cipher:** As the player uncovers data, the map draws connecting lines representing the circular payments washing through shell companies. Hovering over a line reveals raw data: BSBs, account numbers, and Swift codes.
* **The Chain of Custody:** The map explicitly links physical locations to digital servers. It acts as a "laser pointer" showing exactly where specific crimes occurred (like the "two rods over rigger" email on the Westside project).

**The Psychological Function:**
The Nexus Map is the ultimate "Proceeds of Crime Shortcut". The game uses this UI to empower the player. When the player uses the map to freeze an account or track a lie, it works perfectly. It builds an unbreakable contract of trust between the player and the data.

**The Subversion:**
When the player reaches the final terminal, they use the logic and data they gathered from the Nexus Map to execute the decryption. The map didn't lie to them—the data was perfectly accurate. But because the player was primed by the social media ARG ("Silence is the trauma"), their *interpretation* of that perfect data was fundamentally flawed.

They used Ethel's flawless mathematics to execute Dominic's structural psychopathy. They poured their own hubris into the machine.
